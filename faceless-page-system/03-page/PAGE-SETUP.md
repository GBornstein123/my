## 1. USERNAME

### Rules being applied
- X handle limit: **15 characters max**, minimum 4. Allowed: letters, numbers, underscore only.
  No hyphens, no periods, no spaces. Not case-sensitive for uniqueness, but capitalization *displays*
  as you type it — so use CamelCase for readability.
- Excluded by the brief: personal names (`@JakeFinance`), and generic money-page names
  (`@WealthDaily`, `@MoneyMoves`, `@TaxHacksHQ`). Both fail — the first fakes a person who doesn't
  exist, the second signals "generic finance slop account" and attracts the wrong follower.
- The handle should make the niche legible **in a retweet, with no context**. Someone seeing it in
  their timeline should know, before reading the post, whether it's for them.

### RECOMMENDED: `@1099TaxOrder` (12 characters)

Three reasons:
1. **"1099" does the targeting for free.** It is a number nobody types by accident. Anyone who has
   received one recognizes it instantly; anyone who hasn't scrolls past. That is exactly the filter
   this page wants.
2. **"Order" is the product thesis in one word.** The spine's buyer is "not looking for education —
   they are looking for a sequence." The handle promises sequence. Every other candidate promises
   *topic*.
3. **12 characters leaves 3 in reserve.** If `@1099TaxOrder` is taken, `@1099TaxOrder_`,
   `@The1099TaxOrder` (16 — ❌ too long), or `@1099TaxOrders` (13) are available shapes. Handles at
   exactly 15 have no fallback path.

**The one risk, and its mitigation:** "Order" can misparse as "place an order" or as an organization
("The Order of…"). The display name kills that ambiguity in the same eyeline.

### Display name (separate from the handle)

X display names allow up to **50 characters** and may include spaces, punctuation, and emoji.

**Recommended display name: `1099 Taxes, In Order`** (20 characters)

- Puts a space in "1099 Taxes" so it reads as a subject, which the handle can't do.
- "In Order" resolves the handle's ambiguity immediately and restates the promise.
- Contains no name, no credential, no title. Nothing to fake.

Alternates:
- `The 1099 Tax Order` (18) — cleaner, but leaves the ambiguity unresolved.
- `1099 Taxes — What To Do, In What Order` (38) — most explicit; slightly long for mobile, where the
  display name truncates in some list views.
- `1099 Taxes, In Order` + no emoji, no "🔒", no "📈", no flag. **Do not add emoji to the display
  name.** Emoji in a finance display name is the strongest single signal of a low-trust money account.

**Never use in the display name:** "CPA," "EA," "Tax Pro," "Accountant," "Expert," "Advisor,"
"Official." All of these either imply a credential or imply professional certification, and all are
banned by the spine. "Guide," "Notes," "Order," and "Playbook" are safe — they describe a document,
not a person.

## 2. PROFILE PHOTO SPEC

### Technical constraints
- Upload **400 × 400 px minimum** (use 800 × 800 for retina). Square canvas.
- X renders it as a **circle**. Everything outside the inscribed circle is cut. On an 800 × 800 canvas,
  keep all meaningful content inside a **centered 560 px circle** (70%) — the inscribed circle is
  800 px wide, but the visual edge crowds badly past 70%.
- It displays at roughly **48 px** in the timeline. That is the real design constraint: it must be
  legible as a 48 px circle. If you can't read it at thumbnail size, it has failed regardless of how
  it looks at full size.
- Formats: JPG, PNG, GIF (static). Max 2 MB.

### Concept A — "The Form Number" ← recommended

**Build:** Solid flat background, no gradient. Centered, the four numerals **1099** set in a heavy
geometric sans (Canva: *Archivo Black*, *Poppins ExtraBold*, or *Anton*). Tight letter-spacing
(-2%). Numerals occupy ~62% of the canvas width, optically centered (nudge up ~2% — numerals sit
visually low when mathematically centered). No border, no ring, no shadow, no icon.

**Colors:**
- Background: `#111418` (near-black, slight blue cast — reads as ink, not as pure-black default)
- Numerals: `#F4F1EA` (warm paper white — pure `#FFFFFF` on black reads harsh and cheap at 48 px)
- Optional: set the final `9` in `#C8791E` (amber) as a single accent, or leave monochrome.

**Why it reads credible, not generic:** It is a *fact*, not a claim. The account isn't asserting
expertise, status, or wealth — it's stating its subject. This is how reference publications and
document-first brands mark themselves. At 48 px, "1099" is still legible, and it is a number that
performs instant audience segmentation in a crowded timeline. It also carries zero fabrication risk:
there is nothing in it to be untrue.

### Concept B — "The Four Quarters"

**Build:** Solid background. Four equal squares in a single horizontal row, dead center, each square
~14% of canvas width with a gap of ~5% between them. The **first two are filled solid**, the third
is an outline only (2.5% stroke), the fourth is an outline only. No text at all.

**Colors:**
- Background: `#F4F1EA` (paper)
- Filled squares: `#0F3D3A` (deep pine/ink teal)
- Outline squares: `#0F3D3A` at 2.5% stroke on transparent
- Optional third-square outline in `#B3382C` (deadline red) to imply "this one is next"

**Why it reads credible:** It's a progress indicator for the four estimated-tax payment periods —
meaningful to anyone in the niche, invisible to everyone else, and it never says a word. It reads
like a chart from a document rather than a logo from a template. Because it's pure geometry it
survives the 48 px crop better than any lettermark. Weakest point: it requires the bio and banner to
carry all the explicit signaling, since the avatar alone won't tell a cold visitor the topic.

### Concept C — "The Set-Aside Bar"

**Build:** Solid background. A single vertical rounded rectangle, centered, ~28% canvas width and
~58% canvas height, corner radius ~8% of its own width. It is filled from the **bottom up to roughly
one-third**, with a clean horizontal boundary. Thin 2% stroke outlining the whole rectangle. The
filled portion is a different color from the stroke.

**Colors:**
- Background: `#111418`
- Rectangle stroke: `#F4F1EA` at 2% width
- Fill (lower third): `#C8791E` (amber)
- Empty upper portion: background color, not a lighter tint

**Why it reads credible:** It is the mental model of the entire product — money set aside, physically
partitioned from money you can spend. It is abstract enough to avoid looking like clip art and
concrete enough to feel like a diagram. **Critical caveat:** the fill height must be visually
indeterminate — roughly a third, not precisely 30%. If it reads as a specific percentage, it becomes
an unverified numeric claim about a set-aside rate, which the spine forbids without a cited source.
Keep it at an ambiguous fraction and never label it.

### What to avoid — non-negotiable

| Avoid | Why |
|---|---|
| **Any stock photo of a person** — smiling, in a headset, at a laptop, from behind, silhouetted | Violates "faceless." A stock human implies a person who doesn't exist. Reverse-image-searchable, and getting caught destroys the account. |
| **Generic dollar signs** (`$`, `💵`, coins, stacks of bills, piggy banks, money bags) | Signals "generic money page" — the exact category the brief excludes. Also attracts crypto/forex bot followers, which poisons the audience for a $47 information product. |
| **AI-looking gradients** — purple→blue mesh, glossy 3D blobs, "SaaS logo" swooshes, neon glow | Reads as a 2024-era template. Instantly signals no real product behind it. Flat solid color outperforms every gradient at 48 px. |
| **Anything resembling a government seal** — eagles, shields, "official" navy + gold, Treasury-style typography, anything IRS-adjacent | Legally and ethically dangerous: implies official affiliation. The spine bans implied certification; implying you're a *government* source is worse. |
| **A briefcase, a graduation cap, a checkmark badge, a shield with a lock** | Each implies credential, authority, or security guarantee. All are claims. None are true. |
| **Initials or a monogram** (`JB`, `MK`) | A monogram is a person's initials — it implies a name. Faceless means nameless too. |
| **A calculator icon** | The most-used icon in this niche. Not wrong, just invisible. |
| **Fine detail, thin strokes, drop shadows, or more than 3 colors** | Dies at 48 px. |

**Consistency rule:** whatever colors the avatar uses, the banner and every content graphic must use
the same hex values. A page that uses one palette everywhere looks like a publication; one that uses
a different palette per asset looks assembled from templates.

## 3. BANNER SPEC

### How X crops banners — read this before designing

- Upload at **1500 × 500 px** (3:1). Max 2 MB, JPG/PNG.
- X does **not** publish an official safe-area diagram, and the crop behavior has changed multiple
  times across app versions. Treat the numbers below as a conservative envelope, then **verify on an
  actual phone and an actual desktop browser before you consider the page done.**
- **Desktop:** displays close to the full 3:1 image. The profile avatar overlaps the **bottom-left**,
  hanging roughly half below the banner's lower edge.
- **Mobile:** the banner is scaled to the device width and the visible band is effectively
  **shorter** than 3:1 — you lose material from the **top and bottom**. The avatar overlap on the
  bottom-left is proportionally **larger**, and on some layouts the whole left edge is compromised.
- **Both:** X applies a subtle darkening overlay to the banner in some views. Do not rely on delicate
  low-contrast detail.

### The safe-area envelope (use these numbers)

On a 1500 × 500 canvas:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐  y=0
│  DEAD ZONE — top 90px, cropped on mobile               │
├──────────┬─────────────────────────────────────────────┤  y=90
│          │                                             │
│ AVATAR   │        SAFE ZONE                            │
│ EXCLUSION│        x: 420 → 1400                        │
│ x:0→400  │        y: 90  → 380                         │
│ y:250→500│        (980 × 290 usable)                   │
│          │                                             │
├──────────┴─────────────────────────────────────────────┤  y=380
│  DEAD ZONE — bottom 120px, cropped on mobile + avatar  │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘  y=500
```

- **All type and all meaning goes inside x 420–1400, y 90–380.**
- Anything in the left 400 px is decoration only — assume it is covered by the avatar.
- Anything in the top 90 px or bottom 120 px is background only — assume it is cropped.
- Center-weight the composition. If the banner still communicates when you crop it to the middle
  1000 × 290, it will survive every device.

### OPTION 1 — "The Sequence" ← recommended

**Layout:** Left 400 px: solid background, empty (avatar sits here). Right 1080 px: three short lines
of left-aligned text, stacked, starting at x=440. Vertically centered in the safe zone.

**Exact copy:**

```
What to set aside.
When to send it.
What you can deduct.
```

**Type treatment:**
- Typeface: a single sans across all three lines — Canva: *Inter*, *Archivo*, or *Poppins*.
  One weight only: **SemiBold (600)**. Do not mix weights; do not use italics.
- Size: ~62 px cap height per line on the 1500 × 500 canvas (roughly 78 px font size).
- Line height: 1.35. Left-aligned, ragged right.
- Letter-spacing: 0 to -1%. Never positive tracking — spaced-out caps reads as a wellness brand.
- Sentence case with the periods kept. The periods are doing work: they make each line a finished
  statement rather than a slogan fragment.
- Optional: set line 3 in the accent color, lines 1–2 in paper white. Or leave all three monochrome
  and put the accent in a 6 px vertical rule at x=440, running the full height of the text block.

**Palette (exact hex):**
- Background: `#111418`
- Type: `#F4F1EA`
- Accent (rule or third line): `#C8791E`
- If you want a second accent for deadline-themed graphics later: `#B3382C`

**Why it works:** it is the product's table of contents, in the order the buyer needs it. It makes no
claim, promises no outcome, names no credential, and every word of it is true of the guide. A visitor
who reads three lines knows in two seconds whether this page is about their exact problem.

### OPTION 2 — "The Four Dates"

**Layout:** Left 400 px empty. Right side: a single small-caps label line, then a horizontal row of
four date blocks separated by thin vertical rules, then one line of body text underneath.

**Exact copy:**

```
ESTIMATED TAX DEADLINES

APR 15   │   JUN 15   │   SEP 15   │   JAN 15

Nobody withholds these for you.
```

**Type treatment:**
- Label line: same sans, **Medium (500)**, ~26 px, all caps, letter-spacing +8%, color `#C8791E`.
  This is the one place positive tracking is correct — it's a label, not a headline.
- Date row: **Bold (700)**, ~58 px, color `#F4F1EA`. Separators: 2 px vertical rules in `#F4F1EA`
  at 35% opacity, 44 px tall, optically centered to the numerals.
- Body line: **Regular (400)**, ~32 px, color `#F4F1EA` at 80% opacity.
- Even spacing between the four blocks; the row must read as a timeline, not a menu.

**Palette:** identical to Option 1. Same four hex values, no additions.

**Why it works:** it is the single most anxiety-producing fact in the niche, presented as neutral
information. It also gives the page a seasonal heartbeat — the banner can stay identical year-round
while content re-references it four times a year.

**⚠️ VERIFY BEFORE PUBLISH:** those four dates are the *general* estimated-tax due dates associated
with Form 1040-ES, but they **shift when a date falls on a weekend or a legal holiday**, and specific
years have had disaster-relief postponements. Before using Option 2, confirm the exact dates for the
tax year you're publishing in against the current **IRS Form 1040-ES instructions** and
**IRS Publication 505**, and set a reminder to re-check each January. **If you are not willing to
maintain this, use Option 1** — Option 1 contains no date, no number, and no figure that can expire.

That maintenance burden is the reason Option 1 is recommended over Option 2.

### Banner rules that apply to both options
- No photograph. No stock imagery. No screenshot of a spreadsheet or a tax return (a real one leaks
  data; a fake one is fabrication).
- No logos of the IRS, of any tax software, or of Gumroad.
- No URL text in the banner. Banners are not clickable — a typed-out URL there is dead weight and
  looks like a 2016 page. The link field handles this.
- No "DM me," no "link below" arrow pointing at nothing, no "★★★★★."
- Maximum two typefaces, and one is plenty.

## 5. LINK STRATEGY

### The URL field: what to put there

**Put the Gumroad product page URL directly.** Nothing between the profile and the checkout.

```
https://[yourgumroadusername].gumroad.com/l/[product-permalink]
```

Fill both bracketed values from your live Gumroad product's "Share" tab **after** the product is
published, then click the link from a logged-out browser and a phone to confirm it loads the buy page
and not a 404 or a draft preview.

Field mechanics:
- X's website field accepts up to ~100 characters and requires a valid URL. It renders as a shortened,
  clickable display (X strips `https://` and often truncates the path in the display, while the
  underlying link still works).
- **Include `https://`** when you paste it. Some clients fail to linkify a bare domain.
- **Do not add UTM parameters unless you will actually read them.** Gumroad records referrer data on
  its own, which is enough to see X-sourced sales. If you do want explicit tagging, one clean
  parameter is the maximum: `?utm_source=x`. Long UTM strings make the displayed link look like spam
  and cost you clicks.
- If you own a domain, a short branded redirect (e.g. `1099taxorder.com` → the Gumroad page) is
  strictly better looking and gives you the ability to change the destination later without editing
  the profile. This is optional and can wait until after the first sales.

### The case for direct-to-product over Linktree or a homepage

**Against Linktree (and Beacons, Stan, Carrd link-pages):**

1. **Every hop costs conversions.** Profile → Linktree → product is one more page load, one more
   decision, and one more chance to leave. The visitor already decided to click; a menu asks them to
   decide again.
2. **A Linktree signals "creator," not "product."** In this niche the visitor is anxious and looking
   for a source that seems competent and boring. A page of colorful buttons — Instagram, TikTok, "my
   free ebook," "book a call" — reads as a personal brand funnel, which is precisely what a faceless
   page is not.
3. **A link page needs links.** A one-item Linktree is worse than no Linktree. This page has exactly
   one destination, so the menu has nothing to do.
4. **You lose control of the impression.** Linktree renders its own branding and its own domain, which
   dilutes a page whose entire credibility rests on looking like a single-purpose publication.

**Against a homepage / landing page of your own:**

1. **Gumroad's product page is already a landing page** — headline, description, price, buy button,
   ratings, refund policy, and a checkout that works on mobile. Building a second one is duplicated
   work that must be kept in sync.
2. **A separate site is a maintenance liability you don't need at launch**, and any drift between
   your site's copy and the Gumroad page's copy is a trust leak.
3. **Gumroad handles payment, delivery, VAT/sales-tax collection, and receipts.** A homepage that
   just redirects to Gumroad adds a hop for zero benefit; a homepage that sells directly means
   rebuilding all of that.

**The one legitimate exception:** if you later add an email list, the correct structure is *still not*
a Linktree — it's to keep the profile link pointing at the product and put the email opt-in inside the
content (a reply, a periodic post, or the product's own thank-you page). Split the profile link only
when the email list is generating more revenue than the direct link, and even then, test it.

**Also do this:** put the link in the **pinned post** too, not just the bio. On mobile the bio link is
small and easy to miss; a link in the pinned post is a much larger tap target. That's two paths to one
destination, which is fine — two paths to two destinations is what causes the leak.

### P1 — "The two numbers you were given" (265 chars) ← RECOMMENDED

```
You went 1099 this year. Nobody withheld anything.

You've read "set aside 25%" and "set aside 40%" and don't know which applies to you.

You've heard "quarterly taxes" and don't know if you already missed one.

That's the guide. What to do, in what order, by when.
```

**Why it works:** it names three conditions that are simultaneously true of the target buyer and of
almost nobody else. The contradictory-percentage line is the single most reliable recognition trigger
in this niche — the spine's buyer portrait says they got answers "between 15% and 40%," and seeing
that contradiction named back to them is the moment they think *this is my exact situation*. The
"already missed one" line adds urgency without a deadline claim that could expire.
